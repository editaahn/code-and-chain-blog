Last week pushed Web3 teams to make complex rights, risks, and control boundaries legible to users. Soneium laid out a tokenized IP stack for registering creative works, expressing licenses, and distributing revenue. A compromised performance-monitoring script used active Polymarket sessions to sell positions and move funds. Threshold paused the tBTC bridge, and Aave limited new exposure. Meanwhile, Ethena Pay put a leveraged stablecoin strategy behind a one-click savings interface, and Uniswap opened its fee-conversion system to other applications.

This brief covers consequential developments from October 26–November 1, 2026, in Korea Standard Time.

## 1. Soneium turns tokenized IP into a product stack

On October 26, Soneium, the OP Stack network built by Sony Group and Startale Group, [set out a new direction around tokenized intellectual property](https://soneium.org/en/blog/onchain-entertainment-economy). Its Soneium Portal begins with primary sales and is intended to expand into fan participation and royalty distribution. The project describes three product forms: IP Tokens tied to a creator, studio, or title; IP Vaults offering exposure to pools such as music catalog funds; and IP Bonds backed by entertainment businesses. Moriusa, a character produced by Super State Holdings, is its first IP Token, while a DayOneDream partnership is intended to bring K-pop concert royalties onchain.

The more consequential proposal sits below those products. Soneium's planned architecture combines an IP Registry for origin and rights metadata, an IP Graph that links originals with derivatives and collaborations, and an execution layer for licenses, revenue splits, and royalty payments. It also supports [ERC-8432, a proposed machine-readable interface for IP registries and licenses](https://docs.soneium.org/docs/vision-paper/key-initiatives). That could let applications and AI agents query terms and settle permitted uses without depending on one marketplace's database.

An onchain record does not by itself prove copyright ownership or make an offchain claim enforceable. Soneium explicitly says the system is meant to complement existing copyright frameworks, and its announcement describes a vision whose details may change. Applications building on it will need to separate registry assertions from authoritative legal agreements and model the parts that simple ownership tokens omit: rights type, territory, exclusivity, duration, permitted derivatives, royalty splits, version history, and revocation or dispute status. Provenance becomes a product workflow, not just a token URI.

The asset types also require sharper wallet and interface semantics. A fandom collectible, a fungible in-experience currency, and a claim on entertainment cash flows carry different liquidity, eligibility, and regulatory constraints even when they share the same chain and portal. Wallets should identify the rights or economic claim a token actually conveys, preserve the controlling agreement, and avoid implying that transfer of the token transfers the underlying copyright. Settlement systems need reconciliation for disputed or corrected metadata, failed payouts, and offchain revenue reports. Soneium is notable because it is attempting to connect all of those layers; its adoption will depend on whether the legal and operational state remains as legible as the onchain state.

## 2. A monitoring script turns active Polymarket sessions into a withdrawal path

On October 29, a third-party performance-monitoring script loaded by Polymarket was replaced with code that targeted its users. According to [Polymarket's postmortem](https://status.polymarket.com/cmvvek1i90eme14ruga1z6tuj), the script used active browser sessions to sell positions and move funds from wallets for users who had the site open during the attack window. The company paused trading and its relayer, cleared caches, reset all active login sessions, and restored the web app after a 37-minute major outage.

Polymarket said it would return $2.7 million taken in the attack plus $1.2 million for positions and pause-related losses. It identified eligible wallets from onchain and trading data rather than requiring a claim. The incident did not require a failure in the market contracts themselves: trusted JavaScript running inside an authenticated page already had enough context to invoke sensitive application flows.

That distinction matters for every dApp that treats a connected wallet as its security boundary. A transaction simulation, wallet prompt, or contract audit cannot protect users when the page that constructs the action is hostile and the session or smart-wallet policy lets it proceed. High-value dApps should keep nonessential third-party scripts off authenticated trading and signing surfaces, isolate the scripts they retain, pin versions, and enforce a restrictive Content Security Policy. Polymarket said it was redeploying third-party scripts with stronger CSP rules, explicit version pinning, sandboxing, and continuous integrity checks.

The refund process also exposed a product requirement that security plans often omit. A trading platform needs enough event history to reconstruct not just direct drains but losses created by forced sales, slippage, and an emergency pause that spans market resolution. Incident accounting, relayer controls, session invalidation, and customer remediation should be designed together rather than assembled after an attack.

## 3. Threshold pauses tBTC's bridge while lending markets limit exposure

Threshold Network paused new BTC deposits, tBTC minting, and new Bitcoin redemption requests on October 31 while it performed security maintenance. The [precautionary pause](https://governance.aave.com/t/tbtc-mint-redeem-pause-aaves-response/25965) temporarily removed the direct path between the ERC-20 asset and native Bitcoin, even though tBTC could continue circulating on EVM networks.

Aave responded by [freezing tBTC on its V3 Ethereum Core market](https://governance.aave.com/t/tbtc-mint-redeem-pause-aaves-response/25965). The action blocked new tBTC deposits and prevented tBTC from adding borrowing power, while existing users could still repay, withdraw, and undergo liquidation. Aave left the asset's liquidation threshold unchanged, so the control did not reduce existing positions' health factors. Its Base and Arbitrum tBTC reserves had already been frozen through earlier deprecation work.

The episode illustrates why an onchain reserve balance and a working redemption path are different guarantees. A wrapped asset can remain fully backed while losing the mechanism that arbitrageurs use to close a discount, and that can weaken secondary-market liquidity exactly when lenders need to liquidate collateral. Wallets and DeFi interfaces should therefore expose mint, transfer, and redemption availability as separate states instead of presenting one generic network status. Bridge integrations also need durable receipts and idempotent recovery: Threshold specifically told users with pending operations to retain transaction evidence and not retry by sending BTC again.

For lending protocols, an asset-level freeze is less disruptive than a market-wide pause, but only if governance or guardian transactions are prepared before an incident. Risk systems should monitor bridge state, redemption depth, and secondary liquidity alongside proof of reserves, then define how and when new collateral capacity returns after service resumes.

## 4. Ethena Pay puts an 11x stablecoin loop behind a one-click Earn+ balance

Ethena Pay launched Earn+ on October 28, offering a target reward rate of up to 12% through an external vault. The [underlying strategy](https://pay.ethena.fi/blog/earn-launch) supplies USDe to Aave V4 on Avalanche, borrows USDT, acquires more USDe, and repeats the loop to target approximately 11x leverage. Upshift provides the vault infrastructure, Steakhouse curates the strategy, and Ethena Pay provides the interface. The product launched with a $4.5 million vault cap and per-user limits of $20,000, $50,000, or $200,000 depending on account tier.

The interface describes the allocation in dollars, but it does not turn the strategy into a deposit account. Ethena Pay's [Earn+ FAQ](https://pay.ethena.fi/faq) says the reward rate can change, the assets are not insured or guaranteed, and withdrawals are processed rather than instant. The normal target is within 24 hours. Ethena Pay also states that it does not operate, custody, or manage the external vault.

This is a useful test of how much risk an abstraction can hide before it becomes misleading. A one-click flow spans USDe's backing and market price, USDT borrowing liquidity, Aave oracle and liquidation behavior, Avalanche availability, vault administration, and the app's own accounting. The 11x loop magnifies small changes in funding cost, collateral value, or liquidation parameters. A balance screen should therefore distinguish the user's ordinary spendable USD balance from the vault claim, show that withdrawal is a workflow with a processing state, and identify the operators that can change strategy parameters or terms.

Teams embedding similar yield products should calculate the displayed return after borrow costs and performance fees, propagate rate and capacity changes without stale caching, and test stressed exits rather than only successful deposits. "No lock-up" should describe the absence of a contractual term, not imply guaranteed immediate liquidity.

## 5. Uniswap opens its posted-price fee converter to any application

Uniswap Labs released TJAR on October 29 as reusable infrastructure for applications that collect fees in many assets but want to receive one settlement token. An operator deploys a Token Jar and a Releaser, selects the output token, release threshold, and destination, then points fee flows at the jar. Searchers provide the fixed threshold amount and receive the jar's contents when the trade becomes profitable. Uniswap says [TJAR is live on seven chains](https://blog.uniswap.org/tjar-onchain-fee-conversion-for-any-app), and Robinhood uses it to convert Uniswap API fees into USDC and USDG.

TJAR is not an oracle-priced swap or a continuous auction. Its [posted-price mechanism](https://blog.uniswap.org/how-tjar-works-uniswaps-onchain-fee-conversion-system) intentionally leaves a surplus for the searcher. Lower thresholds clear fees more often and reduce price exposure, but a large deposit can push the jar far above the fixed release amount and transfer more value to the first searcher. Higher thresholds reduce that jump risk but keep volatile or illiquid assets on the application's balance sheet for longer.

The contracts let an owner or delegated setter change the output token, threshold, and destination, or permanently relinquish those controls. That makes configuration authority part of treasury security. Integrators should monitor jar value and release efficiency, constrain destination changes with multisig or timelock policy, and decide how to handle tokens with transfer fees, blacklists, or little executable liquidity. TJAR's deterministic multichain deployment can simplify fee addresses, but identical addresses do not eliminate chain-specific asset behavior, finality, or operational risk.

The broader shift is from teams running bespoke swap bots to publishing an onchain conversion opportunity. That removes a keeper from the application's trusted stack, but it replaces a scheduled execution policy with economic incentives. Teams still need alerts for jars that stop clearing, thresholds that leak excessive value, and destinations that receive the right token on the wrong chain.
