## 1. Base: Cobalt Mainnet Upgrade

Base completed the [Cobalt mainnet upgrade](https://status.base.org/) on September 30 at 18:00 UTC. With the core feature of Validity Transactions, users can attach conditions such as expiration time, block height, Flashblock position, and contract storage values to signed transactions. **The sequencer holds these transactions and includes them in a block only when all conditions are met**. Transactions submitted via `base_sendRawTransactionValidity` are not exposed to the public mempool until execution, and if conditions are never met, they remain unexecuted. Previously, Keepers had to monitor the state and compete for submission, but now block builders directly assess eligibility.

The [Cobalt release notes from Base](https://blog.base.dev/cobalt) describe this feature as useful for conditional swaps and withdrawals. It eliminates polling infrastructure and resubmission competition but does not lock the rest of the chain's state at the time of signing. Therefore, wallets and dApps must clearly show users the execution conditions, expiration times, and the possibility of eligible transactions being outbid in gas fees. Additionally, when managing transaction lifecycles, it is advisable to categorize them into states such as submitted, pending, eligible, block-included, and expired, rather than treating the initial signature as a completed order.

Cobalt also expands Base's **native token standard, B20**. Issuers can combine existing KYC whitelists and sanction blacklists using AND/OR logic, reserve changes in display quantity multipliers for corporate actions like stock splits, and activate the `seizeWithMemo` feature if needed. This last feature allows an approved administrator to forcibly transfer a specific holder's balance to another address while recording the reason on-chain. These primitive functions bring regulatory compliance and recovery processes for stablecoins and tokenized assets to the protocol surface but form a completely different permission model from typical ERC-20s. Wallets, indexers, and explorers must clearly expose account freezing and seizure functions, distinguish between actual and multiplier-applied display balances, and update systems to match the new ABI.

## 2. SEC: Custody Regulation Proposal for Advisory Firms and Regulated Funds

On October 1, the SEC proposed [new custody regulations for registered investment advisers and regulated funds](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-100-sec-proposal-would-address-how-investment-advisers-funds-can-custody-crypto-assets-under-federal). This acknowledges that existing qualified custodians may not support new crypto assets that funds wish to handle. According to the proposal, qualified state trust companies[^1] can participate as custodians, and if no qualified custodian can be found, advisers or funds may directly custody the assets. This is not a finalized rule and will undergo a 60-day comment period after publication in the Federal Register.

Self-custody here does not mean individual investors managing their personal wallets. It refers to advisers or funds acting as custodians for client assets. Commissioner Hester Peirce explained that before taking custody, it must be confirmed that no qualified custodian is available, and [this must be reassessed quarterly](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/peirce-statement-proposed-amendments-custody-rules-100126). State trust companies must undergo initial and annual due diligence regarding their licensing status and security measures. Additionally, if advisers or funds directly take custody, they must meet stringent requirements for expertise, cybersecurity, annual audits, internal reporting, account statement issuance, and client disclosures.

If this framework is adopted, the role of institutional custody products will extend beyond simple key storage. They must provide evidence of custody capability for each asset, have internal custody approval processes, conduct quarterly reassessments, and manage incident records. Platforms transferring assets to state trust companies must continuously verify the licensing status and control of these institutions. As custody paths diversify, the importance of wallet policies, key generation and management ceremonies, role division, and auditable approval processes will inevitably grow.

## 3. Roughrider: Integrating Solana-based Bank Tokens with Existing Core Banking Systems

On October 1, Fiserv announced the opening of its digital asset platform to financial institution clients, with North Dakota Bank's 'Roughrider Coin' [becoming the first real-use case](https://investors.fiserv.com/news-releases/news-release-details/fiserv-digital-asset-platform-goes-live-financial-institution). Roughrider Coin is a prime example of integrating the fast speed of public chains with strict participant restrictions and recovery controls into core banking workflows.

Over 90 banks and credit unions in North Dakota can use it for interbank fund transfers. VersaBank USA handles issuance, burning, reserve management, and custody, Fireblocks provides digital asset infrastructure, and Solana processes transactions. Participating institutions conduct transactions using the existing 'Fiserv Commercial Center' interface for interbank fund transfers.

According to [North Dakota Bank's architecture description](https://bnd.nd.gov/roughrider/), this token is not a general retail public stablecoin. It is a permissioned, institution-only token matched 1:1 with the US dollar. It uses Solana's Token-2022 control features, including account freezing and clawback functions, and participating institutions trade through Fireblocks MPC wallets with automated compliance policies. Token minting occurs only after cash is deposited from the operating account of a participating institution to a designated For Benefit Of (FBO) account. When tokens reach the recipient institution's wallet, a smart contract command automatically burns the tokens.

In this structure, on-chain finality and the final bank ledger state operate separately. Besides token transfers and automatic burning, funds between FBO accounts are net settled daily and moved via ACH files[^2]. Therefore, the system should not immediately consider the success status on a blockchain explorer as final settlement completion.

## 4. MetaMask's Default Security Settings Reveal Metadata Exposure and Infrastructure Dependency Issues

On September 29, MetaMask integrated profile and privacy control features into a single default setting. [This setting is enabled by default for new users](https://metamask.io/en-GB/news/basic-functionality-profile-setting) and supports token and NFT detection, transaction simulation, security alerts, phishing prevention, ENS domain resolution, contract decoding, and network status checks. In return, wallet addresses are linked to individual profiles, and standard network requests are sent when some features operate. Wallets using the Secret Recovery Phrase method can disable this setting but will lose access to these features. In contrast, wallets created through social logins like Google, Apple, and Telegram are tied to the login model itself, preventing the setting from being disabled. This is separate from the optional anonymous metrics collection setting.

The default-enabled security protection features reduce phishing and blind signing incidents but make linking addresses and profiles a default behavior. While self-custody may guarantee control over private keys, it does not conceal the metadata needed for token detection, simulation, and security alert backends. Wallet services must clearly inform users, even outside the privacy menu, about which addresses and requests are sent to which services before account creation, what exactly is disabled when the setting is turned off, and how social login methods differ from the Secret Recovery Phrase method.

During the same week, a separate operational infrastructure dependency issue arose. From September 30, MetaMask [proactively removed affected validators among non-custodial staking nodes](https://metamask.io/en-GB/news/user-update) in response to a security incident impacting part of its infrastructure. As of October 1, MetaMask stated there was no indication of impact on wallets or customer funds, and that it does not manage customers' withdrawal keys. Since the company has not disclosed specific causes, one should not hastily speculate on attack vectors or impacts. However, this incident highlights that key custody and service availability are distinctly separate boundaries. Even if funds remain safe under user control during staking, simulation, alert, and backend-based functions can be degraded. Therefore, status notifications and incident response processes must report these states separately.

## 5. Ethereum Glamsterdam Upgrade: Testing ePBS and Parallel Validation on Sepolia

The Ethereum Foundation announced on September 28 that [Glamsterdam will be activated on Sepolia on October 6 at 13:53:36 UTC](https://blog.ethereum.org/en/2026/09/17/glamsterdam-testnet-announcement). There is no schedule yet for Hoodi and the mainnet. Glamsterdam includes changes to the Amsterdam execution layer and the Gloas consensus layer. The core features are proposer-builder separation (ePBS) and block-level access list (BAL) embedded in the protocol.

This blog details the changes in Glamsterdam. [Read more: Fusaka, Glamsterdam, Hegota: Evolution of Ethereum L1 Core and Scalability Roadmap](/content/blog/ko/261007-fusaka-glamsterdam-hegota)

[^1]:
    **State Trust Company**  
    A financial institution in the U.S. authorized to operate by the financial authorities of a specific state.

[^2]:
    **ACH File**  
    ACH stands for Automated Clearing House, a network used by U.S. banks for bulk transfers. Banks send multiple transfers in a NACHA format file, known as an ACH file, rather than sending money individually in real-time. The receiving bank reads the file to deposit or withdraw from accounts. Typically, it is bundled daily and does not complete in seconds like a chain.
